### Industry standards and regulation (6 searches)
The best-supported finding is the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency rule. From 2 August 2026, AI-generated text published on matters of public interest must be labeled unless a human has reviewed it or has editorial control. For creative or fictional works, the duty shrinks to an appropriate disclosure. That fits StoryML's dramatized format, but it only helps if the team keeps real human editorial review. For the email newsletter, the FTC's official guidance gives concrete US rules if emails promote commercial content: a working opt-out honored within 10 business days, a valid postal address, honest headers and subject lines, and penalties of up to $53,088 per violating email. EU/UK sources say marketing emails generally need consent, and the consent can't be bundled into the terms. Evidence for these areas comes from official sources and is strong. I ran out of searches before covering CCPA thresholds (from an official source), OWASP, NIST, WCAG 2.2, AI copyright or FTC endorsement rules, so those are open questions. The search tool showed page age only as relative days, so I couldn't confirm most publication dates.
